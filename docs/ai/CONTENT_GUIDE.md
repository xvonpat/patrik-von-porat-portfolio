# vonporat.com — Content Guide

Last reviewed: 2026-10-22

## Purpose

This document defines how vonporat.com communicates in public-facing copy.

It covers voice, messaging hierarchy, page copy, project descriptions, blog content, metadata, and editorial standards.

- `AGENTS.md` contains mandatory project rules.
- `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` explains Patrik, the audience, and the meaning of the project.
- `SITE_ARCHITECTURE.md` defines where content belongs.
- `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` defines visual presentation and typography.
- `TECHNICAL_SYSTEM.md` defines CMS and technical boundaries.

## Public language

Public-facing website content is written in **English**.

Internal working notes and conversations may be in Swedish, but public copy remains English. Use natural, precise international English. Avoid inflated vocabulary or artificial attempts to sound grand.

## Core message

Patrik creates music, images, physical work, and digital systems. The connecting idea is not that he has many unrelated interests. It is that he approaches different forms of work with curiosity, structure, craft, and iterative refinement.

The site communicates:
1. A credible 35+ year relationship with music, guitar, and worldbuilding.
2. An active commitment to visual and physical craft (drawing, tattoo practice, miniatures).
3. A thoughtful use of technology and AI-assisted tools.
4. A professional foundation in systems, analytics (Power BI), and process improvement (Lean Six Sigma).
5. Current projects and selected evidence rather than a catalogue of abstract claims.

## Voice & voice balance

### Personal, not self-absorbed
Use first person naturally to explain motivations, observations, roles, and creative choices without turning every paragraph into autobiography.

### Confident, not inflated
State real experience and responsibility directly:
- **Prefer**: *“I have played guitar for more than 35 years, with a background that includes Freternia, Cromonic, Realmforged and Ashwrithe.”*
- **Avoid**: *“A visionary musician with unparalleled expertise across the global metal landscape.”*

### Reflective, not vague
Explain what changed, what was learned, or why a choice was made:
- **Prefer**: *“The more time I spend working digitally, the more important physical craft becomes.”*
- **Avoid**: *“Creativity is an infinite journey without boundaries.”*

### Technical, not jargon-heavy
Use technical terms when they add accuracy, explaining their role in context:
- **Prefer**: *“I use Power BI and process mapping to turn complex operational data into clearer decisions.”*
- **Avoid**: *“Skilled in Power BI, Minitab, SAP, Excel, Notion, Payload, Supabase, Vercel, Pro Tools, and Photoshop.”*

## Writing principles

- **Lead with meaning**: State what something is and why it matters before listing tools or dates.
- **Evidence over adjectives**: Show what was built, recorded, drawn, learned, or improved.
- **Controlled sentences**: Balance short and medium sentences; avoid fragments used only to imitate luxury advertising.
- **One paragraph, one job**: Each paragraph develops one clear thought and transitions naturally.
- **Remove repetition**: Give each section a distinct job rather than repeating the same biographical summary.
- **No dashes as punctuation**: Do not use em dashes (—) or spaced en dashes ( – ) in public copy, alt text or metadata. Patrik reads them as a sign of AI-written text. Rephrase with a comma, colon, full stop or parentheses. En dashes in number ranges (`1998–2023`) are fine.

## Preferred language

Useful recurring concepts:
- Selected work
- Current focus / Active projects
- Creative practice / Traditional craft
- Systems thinking / Structured problem-solving / Process improvement
- Built, tested, learned, refined
- Observe → Structure → Create → Refine

### Language to avoid
- Generic filler: “multidisciplinary creative professional”, “passionate creator”, “pushing boundaries”, “cutting-edge solutions”
- Inflated claims: “master of many disciplines”, “visionary leader”, “revolutionary ecosystem”
- Unverified metrics, client lists, or awards
- Occult or overly dark fantasy language on the general personal site

## Brand boundaries

- **vonporat.com**: Personal creative hub with a human, broad, and structured voice.
- **Realmforged**: Power/symphonic metal, dark fantasy worldbuilding.
- **Ashwrithe**: Dark extreme metal with cold, ritualistic atmosphere.
- Do not transfer Ashwrithe's ritual lore or Realmforged's fantasy copy into general personal-site writing.

## Calls to action

### Preferred patterns
- **Exploration**: `Explore Selected Work`, `View Visual Practice`, `Explore Music`, `Read the Journal`.
- **Listening**: `Listen to Realmforged`, `Enter Ashwrithe`, `Listen on Spotify`.
- **Project links**: `Explore Realmforged`, `View Project`, `About the Process`.
- **Contact**: `Send an Email`, `Start a Conversation`.

### Avoid
- `Click here`, `Submit`, `Learn more` (without context), `Discover the magic`, `Unlock your potential`.

## UI microcopy & error handling

- Microcopy should be direct and clear: `Menu`, `Close`, `Previous image`, `Next image`, `All`, `Filter by category`.
- Error messages state what failed and offer a recovery step without exposing internal stack traces or database errors:
  - *“The article could not be loaded. Please try again.”*
  - *“No notes found in this category.”*

## SEO and metadata copy

- **Page titles**: Clear and specific (e.g. `Selected Work | Patrik von Porat`, `About | Patrik von Porat`).
- **Meta descriptions**: Natural, informative summaries (150–160 chars) accurately reflecting page content.
- **Open Graph**: Consistent title and description with dedicated social preview images.
- **Slugs**: Short, lowercase, hyphenated strings (`/blog/creating-the-ashwrithe-logo`).
- **Alt text**: Descriptive alternative text for content images; empty alt (`alt=""`) for purely decorative images.

## AI drafting procedure

When an AI agent drafts public content:
1. Identify the page, audience, and purpose.
2. Read the relevant project context and verify facts against the codebase.
3. Draft concise, meaningful copy without generic filler or inflated superlatives.
4. Check voice, terminology, and brand boundaries.
5. Review the copy in its intended layout to ensure natural line flow.
