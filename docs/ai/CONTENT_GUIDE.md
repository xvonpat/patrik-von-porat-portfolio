# vonporat.com — Content Guide

Last reviewed: 2026-08-14

## Purpose

This document defines how vonporat.com should communicate in public-facing copy.

It covers voice, messaging hierarchy, page responsibilities, terminology, calls to action, project cards, blog content, metadata, and editorial quality. It is intended for AI agents, editors, and future contributors.

- `AGENTS.md` contains mandatory project rules.
- `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` explains Patrik, the audience, and the meaning of the project.
- `SITE_ARCHITECTURE.md` defines where content belongs.
- `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` defines how content is presented visually.
- This file defines what the site says and how it says it.

Existing published copy is useful evidence but is not automatically canonical. Template content, old drafts, and placeholder text must not override the current direction.

## Public language

Public-facing website content should normally be written in **English** unless Patrik explicitly requests another language.

Internal planning, working notes, and conversations may be Swedish. Do not mix Swedish and English within public page copy unless the language shift is deliberate and approved.

Use natural international English. Avoid unnecessarily complicated vocabulary, idioms that obscure meaning, and artificial attempts to sound native, grand, or literary.

## Canonical positioning

Primary positioning:

> **Patrik von Porat — guitarist, visual artist and systems-minded creator.**

Supporting thought:

> **One identity, many expressions.**

Process bridge:

> **Observe → Structure → Create → Refine**

These lines provide orientation. They should not all be repeated on every page or section.

## Core message

Patrik creates music, images, physical work, and digital systems. The connecting idea is not that he has many unrelated interests. It is that he approaches different forms of work with curiosity, structure, craft, and iterative refinement.

The site should communicate:

1. A long and credible relationship with music and guitar.
2. An active commitment to visual and physical craft.
3. A thoughtful use of technology and AI-assisted tools.
4. A professional foundation in systems, analytics, and improvement.
5. Current projects and selected evidence rather than a catalogue of claims.

## Voice

The vonporat.com voice should be:

- Personal
- Specific
- Calm
- Confident without self-importance
- Thoughtful
- Curious
- Grounded
- Reflective when useful
- Concise enough to scan
- Clear enough for visitors who do not know Patrik

It may be warm, dry, or quietly enthusiastic when the subject supports it. It should not sound like corporate brand copy or a fictional persona.

## Voice balance

### Personal, not self-absorbed

First person is appropriate because this is Patrik's personal hub. Use “I” when describing motivations, roles, observations, and work.

Avoid beginning every paragraph with “I” or turning every project description into autobiography.

### Confident, not inflated

State real experience and responsibility directly.

Prefer:

> I have played guitar for more than 30 years, with a background that includes Freternia, Cromonic, Realmforged and Ashwrithe.

Avoid:

> A visionary musician with unparalleled expertise across the global metal landscape.

### Reflective, not vague

Explain what changed, what was learned, why a choice was made, or how disciplines connect.

Prefer:

> The more time I spend working digitally, the more important physical craft becomes.

Avoid:

> Creativity is a journey where boundaries disappear and possibilities become infinite.

### Technical, not jargon-heavy

Use technical terms when they improve accuracy. Explain their role rather than listing them for status.

Prefer:

> I use Power BI to turn operational data into clearer decisions.

Avoid:

> Skilled in Power BI, Minitab, SAP, Excel, Notion, Payload, Supabase, Vercel, Pro Tools, Photoshop and more.

## Writing principles

### Lead with meaning

Begin with what something is and why it matters. Add tools, methods, or history after the reader is oriented.

### Use concrete nouns and active verbs

Prefer words such as build, draw, write, record, test, map, refine, release, practice, and improve.

Reduce dependence on abstract nouns such as innovation, passion, excellence, synergy, creativity, and transformation when they are not supported by specifics.

### Prefer evidence over adjectives

Do not say a project is ambitious, innovative, professional, unique, or successful when the copy can show what was built, released, learned, or improved.

### Keep sentences controlled

Mix short and medium-length sentences. Use longer sentences only when the relationship between ideas benefits from them.

Avoid fragments used only to imitate luxury-brand copy.

### One paragraph, one job

Each paragraph should establish one idea, develop it, and create a natural transition.

### Remove repetition

When two sections explain the same identity, keep the stronger version and give the other section a different responsibility.

## Brand boundaries

vonporat.com is the personal hub. Realmforged and Ashwrithe are independent projects presented within it.

### vonporat.com voice

- Human
- Personal
- Broad enough to connect several disciplines
- Clear and readable
- Reflective and structured
- Dark in aesthetic, not necessarily dark in every sentence

### Realmforged voice

Realmforged may use fantasy, power-metal, narrative, and release-specific language appropriate to that project. On vonporat.com, describe it clearly before using project terminology.

Do not make all personal-site copy sound like a fantasy campaign.

### Ashwrithe voice

Ashwrithe has a colder, ritualistic, severe, concise, and more impersonal identity. Its canonical project language and lore belong to Ashwrithe-controlled surfaces.

Do not transfer phrases such as “Rituals in rot and winter,” project-specific liturgical language, sealed-content terminology, or blackened genre framing into general vonporat.com copy.

When Ashwrithe appears on the personal site, explain what it is in readable language and link to the dedicated project for deeper immersion.

## Messaging hierarchy

Use the following sequence when introducing Patrik or the site:

1. **Identity** — guitarist, visual artist and systems-minded creator.
2. **What he makes** — music, images, physical work and digital systems.
3. **What connects it** — observation, structure, craft and refinement.
4. **Proof** — selected projects, releases, artwork, practice and systems.
5. **Next step** — explore, listen, view, read or contact.

Do not lead with a long tool list or professional title before establishing the person and work.

- View visual work

The introduction should explain the relationship between disciplines, not repeat the hero title.

Working direction:

Check the surrounding page before using this exact sentence. Remove or adapt any repeated concepts.

### Creative pillars

Pillar labels should be clear:

- Music
- Visual Art
- Projects
- Writing

Descriptions should state what visitors will find rather than make abstract claims.

### Selected Work

Section copy should be minimal. Let project names, images, roles, and real outcomes provide evidence.

Suitable introduction:

### Creative Operating System

Capabilities should be expressed as modes of work:

- Music & Sound
- Visual Craft
- Digital Systems
- Improvement & Insight

Explain what Patrik does within each mode. Do not use a rating system, skill percentage, or unexplained badge wall.

### Process

Use:

> **Observe → Structure → Create → Refine**

If supporting copy is needed, explain the process once. Do not convert each word into inflated brand philosophy.

### Current Focus

Current Focus should be factual, brief, and easy to replace.

Working direction as of this review:

> Currently building Ashwrithe, developing new visual work, refining tattoo technique and shaping digital tools that make creative work easier to manage.

Keep this to two or three active themes when possible. Update it when actual priorities change.

### Latest Blog

Use a direct section title such as:

- Latest writing
- From the journal
- Recent notes

Avoid implying a large publication operation when only a few posts exist.

### Homepage closing CTA

Keep the final invitation restrained.

Example direction:

> Interested in the work, a collaboration, or the systems behind it?

Possible actions:

- Get in touch
- View contact options

## Blog editorial guide

### Role of the blog

The blog is a creative journal and permanent home for context that does not fit in portfolio summaries.

It may document what Patrik is:

- Building
- Releasing
- Drawing
- Practising
- Testing
- Learning
- Improving
- Reconsidering

### Categories

Use this controlled category set:

- Behind the Scenes
- Music
- Visual Art
- Process & Systems
- Tools & Experiments
- Personal

Do not create a new category for a single post when an existing category works. Use tags for narrower subjects.

### Blog voice

Blog posts may be more conversational and reflective than page copy. They should still be edited, structured, and useful to a reader.

Suitable structure:

1. Concrete opening observation or situation
2. Why it mattered
3. What was done, tested, or learned
4. Specific examples or images
5. A concise conclusion or next step

Not every post needs to teach a universal lesson. A well-observed account of current work is enough.

### Titles

Titles should be specific and readable.

Useful patterns:

- Returning to tattoo practice
- Building Ashwrithe's first visual system
- Why more technology makes me value physical craft
- What I learned from rebuilding my personal site
- From raw data to a clearer operational view

Avoid clickbait, false urgency, and generic titles such as “My Creative Journey.”

### Excerpts

An excerpt should state the post's actual subject and give a reason to read. It should not merely repeat the title or begin the article verbatim.

Aim for one or two concise sentences.

### Introductions

Start close to the subject. Avoid several paragraphs of generic context before reaching the event, object, problem, or observation.

### Conclusions

End with the current understanding, unresolved question, next experiment, or natural stopping point. Do not force an inspirational moral.

## UI microcopy

Functional text should prioritize clarity over atmosphere.

Examples:

- Menu
- Close
- Previous image
- Next image
- View full image
- Copy link
- Link copied
- Loading article
- No posts found
- Return to blog
- Page not found
- Try again

Do not make routine controls mysterious or project-themed.

### Error messages

Error copy should:

- State what failed
- Avoid blame
- Offer a useful next step when possible
- Never expose internal errors, paths, identifiers, or secrets

Prefer:

> The article could not be loaded. Please try again.

Avoid:

> Database query failed: connection refused at…

## SEO and metadata copy

SEO content should remain human and page-specific.

### Page title

- Make the subject clear.
- Include Patrik's name or vonporat.com when useful.
- Avoid stuffing several disciplines into every title.

### Meta description

- Summarize the actual page.
- Use natural language.
- Include a useful differentiator.
- Do not promise content that is not present.

### Open Graph text

Use the same positioning as the page. Avoid a second, more promotional voice for social previews.

### Slugs

Use short, descriptive lowercase slugs with hyphens. Do not change established public slugs without considering redirects and existing links.

### Alt text

Describe the image's meaningful content and context. Do not include “image of” unless necessary. Do not use alt text as a keyword field.

Examples:

- Patrik recording bass with a Pro Tools session visible in the background
- Graphite drawing of a horned creature on white paper
- Tattoo practice piece with a skull, flowers and pepper shading on reelskin

Decorative images should use appropriate empty alternative text in implementation.

## Dates, status, and factual precision

- Verify release dates before publishing them.
- Distinguish released, announced, planned, in development, and concept work.
- Do not describe a solo project as a full band unless the current approved project material does so.
- Do not call practice work client work.
- Do not imply certification, employment history, collaborators, features, or credits that cannot be verified.
- Use “more than 30 years” for guitar experience unless a more precise current figure is deliberately maintained.
- Treat current-focus copy as time-sensitive and review it regularly.

## Formatting

### Headings

Use sentence case by default. Project and release names retain official capitalization.

Headings should help scanning and make sense outside the visual design.

### Paragraphs

Keep web paragraphs reasonably short. Break when the subject changes, not after every sentence.

### Lists

Use lists for comparable items, steps, fields, or compact inventories. Do not turn narrative content into bullet lists merely to reduce writing effort.

### Emphasis

Use bold and italic sparingly. Visual hierarchy should not depend on bolding many phrases inside every paragraph.

### Punctuation

- Use em dashes deliberately, not in every sentence.
- Avoid repeated exclamation marks.
- Avoid decorative ellipses.
- Use the arrow form consistently: Observe → Structure → Create → Refine.

## Content review checklist

Before publishing or approving public copy:

- [ ] Is it written in English unless another language was requested?
- [ ] Does the reader understand the subject quickly?
- [ ] Is the copy specific to Patrik or the actual project?
- [ ] Are all claims, roles, dates, links, and results verifiable?
- [ ] Does it avoid generic corporate or portfolio filler?
- [ ] Does it avoid repeating nearby content?
- [ ] Does each paragraph have a clear job?
- [ ] Are project identities kept distinct?
- [ ] Is AI use described honestly and proportionately?
- [ ] Are practice, concept, active, legacy, and released states accurate?
- [ ] Is the CTA clear about what happens next?
- [ ] Are category, tag, title, excerpt, and metadata values consistent?
- [ ] Does the copy fit the available layout without becoming unnaturally compressed?
- [ ] Has spelling of names, tools, and platforms been checked?

## AI drafting procedure

When an AI agent drafts or edits public content:

1. Identify the page, audience, and purpose.
2. Read the relevant project context and any project-specific source material.
3. Inspect existing nearby copy to avoid repetition.
4. Separate verified facts from assumptions.
5. Draft the smallest amount of copy needed for the section.
6. Check voice and brand boundaries.
7. Remove generic claims and unnecessary adjectives.
8. Verify names, roles, status, dates, links, and terminology.
9. Review the copy in its intended layout when possible.
10. Preserve unpublished or private information.

If a required fact is unknown, leave a clear editorial placeholder in internal work or ask Patrik. Do not invent a polished public claim.
