## Project summary

**vonporat.com** is Patrik von Porat's personal creative hub.

It is not intended to be a conventional résumé, a corporate portfolio, a Linktree replacement, or a complete archive of everything Patrik has ever made. It is a curated, evolving overview of selected work, active projects, creative practice, writing, and systems thinking.

The site brings together several areas that might initially appear separate:

- Music and guitar
- Visual art and traditional craft
- Tattoo practice
- Generative and AI-assisted visuals
- Miniatures and 3D printing
- Websites and digital experiments
- Writing and documentation
- Lean Six Sigma and process improvement
- Power BI, analytics, and systems thinking

The central task of the site is to make these areas feel like expressions of one identity rather than an unrelated list of hobbies, tools, or professional skills.

## Canonical positioning

Primary positioning:

> **Patrik von Porat — guitarist, visual artist and systems-minded creator.**

Supporting thought:

> **One identity, many expressions.**

The creative and analytical sides are connected through the working process:

> **Observe → Structure → Create → Refine**

This sequence describes how Patrik approaches music, visual work, digital systems, and improvement work:

- **Observe** — study the subject, environment, problem, reference, or desired feeling.
- **Structure** — identify relationships, constraints, patterns, and a workable direction.
- **Create** — turn the direction into music, imagery, writing, software, or a practical workflow.
- **Refine** — review, test, simplify, improve, and repeat.

## Patrik von Porat

Patrik is a Swedish guitarist, visual artist, creator, and process-improvement practitioner based in Borås, Sweden.

He has played guitar for more than 30 years and has a long background in metal music. His musical history includes Freternia, Cromonic, and other projects, while his current creative focus includes Realmforged and Ashwrithe.

His visual practice includes graphite drawing, tattoo practice, generative visuals, dark illustration, miniatures, and 3D-printed figures. Traditional craft matters because it creates a physical counterweight to digital and AI-assisted work.

Professionally, Patrik works with logistics, distribution, warehouse operations, structured problem-solving, and continuous improvement. He is Lean Six Sigma Green Belt certified and works with tools and methods such as DMAIC, process mapping, Power BI, Minitab, and workflow design.

Technology is not a separate fascination added to the side. It is part of how Patrik builds, documents, connects, and improves creative projects. The same person may work on a guitar arrangement, a graphite drawing, a tattoo exercise, a website structure, or a Power BI model — and approach all of them with depth, precision, curiosity, and iterative refinement.

## Why the site exists

The site should:

1. Give visitors a clear first impression of Patrik.
2. Connect music, art, digital work, and systems thinking.
3. Present selected proof rather than broad claims.
4. Make active projects and current focus visible.
5. Provide useful paths to music, project websites, social platforms, and contact.
6. Give writing and behind-the-scenes work a permanent home.
7. Act as a structured base for Patrik's evolving creative identity.

The site is both outward-facing and personally useful. Publicly, it helps other people understand the work. Internally, it creates a reason to curate, document, and connect projects that would otherwise remain spread across many platforms.

## Public content pillars

Music is one of the site's primary pillars.

Current active focus:

## Selected Work philosophy

The site should show enough proof to feel credible and alive without trying to publish everything.

Selected work should be chosen because it does at least one of the following:

- Represents current direction
- Demonstrates real skill or progress
- Adds useful contrast to another discipline
- Shows how Patrik thinks or works
- Provides a strong destination for further exploration
- Helps explain the overall identity

An unfinished item may be shown when the process itself is the subject, but it should be clearly presented as current practice, work in progress, or exploration.

## Active website direction

The intended public navigation is:

> Home · Music · Art · Projects · Blog · About · Contact

Projects is part of the intended architecture but remains hidden from public navigation until its descriptions, proof cards, images, and links are ready.

The homepage is evolving toward this structure:

1. Clear hero positioning
2. Short introduction
3. Creative pillars
4. Selected Work / Proof Cards
5. Creative Operating System / capabilities
6. Observe → Structure → Create → Refine
7. Now / Current Focus
8. Latest strong blog content
9. Restrained contact or link exit

The homepage should feel like an entry point, not a compressed version of every other page.

## Tone and atmosphere in context

The personal site should feel:

- Dark but readable
- Premium but not corporate
- Artistic but not obscure
- Personal but not diary-like
- Structured but not sterile
- Modern but not trend-dependent
- Technically capable but not presented as a SaaS product

The site may use dark aesthetics and cinematic presentation, but it should remain broader and more human than any single music project's brand.

Realmforged and Ashwrithe each have their own identity. Their language, genre framing, colors, imagery, and lore should remain distinct when presented inside the personal hub.

## Technology in the project story

The site currently uses Next.js, Payload CMS, Supabase/PostgreSQL, Vercel, and GitHub.

Technology supports the project by making it possible to:

- Publish and organize writing
- Maintain media and selected work
- Connect personal and project destinations
- Evolve the site without rebuilding everything
- Experiment with structured creative systems

The technology should support the visitor experience. It should not become the main subject unless a project case or blog post is specifically about the system itself.

## What success looks like

A successful version of vonporat.com should allow a new visitor to answer these questions quickly:

- Who is Patrik von Porat?
- What does he create?
- What is active now?
- What selected work should I explore?
- How do music, visual art, technology, and systems thinking belong together?
- Where can I listen, view more work, read, or make contact?

The site succeeds when it feels like a believable extension of Patrik rather than a template populated with content.
